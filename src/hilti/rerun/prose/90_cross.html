09  the result

The prior holds. The track does not.

Twelve cross-sequence runs, two configurations each, and none of them completes its bag. The longest is site3_handheld_4 in site3's hub map at 71.6 s of 106; the rest end between 4.8 and 38.5 s. This is a negative result, so the question worth answering is which part fails — and it is not the part this report set out to build.

{{tbl_cross2}}

Three things that are working

  • The registration. Nine of twelve priors place the start pose within 6 cm and 1° of a re-fit restricted to the sequence's own neighbourhood, at overlaps of 0.37–0.75. ConSLAM tracked full bags cross-sequence on 0.46–0.66 with the same release, so these are not marginal alignments.
  • The pose, while it lasts. On the two best site2 pairs the estimate sits 23 and 30 cm from the sequence's own FAST-LIO trajectory in the hub's frame, with 80 % of the tracked span inside a metre and inlier ratios of 0.55–0.67. The localizer is genuinely localized in another visit's map. It just cannot stay that way.
  • The gravity prior, once corrected. Section 07's knob matters most here: site2_handheld_4 in the robot map went from 16.44 m off the registered reference to 0.225 m, and from an inlier ratio of 0.096 to 0.628, on that one value. The 0.3 rad it had been carrying was written for un-levelled map frames and is 17° of freedom the filter spends explaining the 10–20 cm disagreement a different visit legitimately shows.

What is not working

The covariance grows without bound and the run is cut — at 200 in configuration A, at 2000 in configuration B, and the earlier own-map experiment showed that removing the limit lets genuine divergence run for hundreds of metres rather than rescuing anything. Five runs never get that far and die on Motion predictor update failed. Every pair was attempted three times and the outcome reproduces to within a few seconds, so this is not the intermittent startup fragility ConSLAM documented; it is the same wall every time.

Two contributing conditions are measurable and neither is sufficient on its own. Route coverage — the overlap in a 15 m ball around each pose along the walk — drops below 0.5 for 16 % to 83 % of the route on five of the twelve pairs, against ConSLAM's 8 % worst case. Site 3's figure shows it directly: the registered routes leave the hub map's footprint. But site2_handheld_4 has a route-coverage minimum of 0.73 and fails anyway, so thin coverage is not the whole explanation.

What separates this from ConSLAM

The same release, the same method and a comparable prior tracked all of ConSLAM's cross-sequence bags end to end at 11 cm median. The differences that remain unexplored here are the ones this dataset adds: indoor multi-level geometry with self-similar stairwells and bays, a 400 Hz rig whose map frames sit ~180° from gravity before levelling, and routes that share a building without sharing a path. Which of those matters is the next question, and this run does not answer it.

What the run does establish

Every stage before the cross-sequence localizer is now sound and reproducible. Fifteen sequences at 99.6–99.9 % of their clouds, with three independent input-fidelity bugs found and fixed; eleven of fifteen odometry runs inside 16 cm of the survey; fifteen levelled maps; own-map localization agreeing with its own odometry to 0.8–2.7 cm on all fifteen and running seven full bags without a single LOST. Against that, cross-sequence reuse on this dataset is currently blocked in kd_localizer's covariance behaviour rather than in the map, the registration or the pipeline — which is a smaller and much better-posed problem than the one this campaign started with.
