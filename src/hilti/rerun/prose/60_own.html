06  localization in its own map

Where it tracks, it tracks to a centimetre. Seven of fifteen reach the end.

Each bag replayed against a .kdm built from its own FAST-LIO cloud — the easy case, and the floor for everything after it. The map holds exactly the geometry the bag is about to see, and the map frame is the rig's start pose, so initial_pose is the gravity levelling and nothing else.

The accuracy result is unambiguous. Against the sequence's own FAST-LIO trajectory, in the frame they share, the median disagreement is 0.8 to 2.7 cm on every one of the fifteen — including the ones that are later declared lost, and including site2_handheld_5, whose map contains a 37 m fold. On the surveyed control points, every sequence that reaches two of them scores 1.9 to 16.3 cm, which is the same band as the odometry that built the map and in four cases slightly better than it.

Tracking is the part that splits. All three robot sequences and all four of site3's handheld sequences run their full bag with zero LOST — 698.6 s of 700 on site2_robot_1, at a covariance factor peaking at 36.9 against a limit of 200. Every site1 sequence and every site2 handheld sequence is declared lost partway, always on the same reason: Pose covariance is too high (Rotation X/Y/Z).

What the losing frame actually looks like

The reason string names a rotation covariance, so the obvious reading is that the attitude estimate diverged. The rows around each loss say otherwise — the absolute covariances there are small, and on one sequence every ray agrees with the map: 

the losing frame, from the monitor CSV
                     reason              inlier   sig_rot     as deg    sig_pos
site1_handheld_2     Rotation Y           0.181   0.00234    0.13 deg    1.5 cm
site1_handheld_1     Rotation X           0.242   0.01426    0.82 deg    2.3 cm
site1_handheld_5     Rotation X           0.224   0.01806    1.03 deg    2.3 cm
site2_handheld_4     Rotation Z           1.000   0.03569    2.04 deg    3.9 cm

site2_handheld_4 is the clear case: a ray inlier ratio of 1.000 — every ray consistent with the map — and 2.0° of rotation uncertainty, declared lost. What the gate thresholds is not the covariance but max_pose_sigma_factor_increase, the ratio of the current sigma to the run's own best. A filter that starts very confident earns a large ratio from a small absolute rise, so the threshold means different things on different sequences. At the losing frame the ratio had reached 254–380 against the limit of 200.

The four short runs were each retried three times and reproduced to within a second — site2_handheld_4 at 16.8, 17.0 and 17.1 s of a 93 s bag — so these are not the intermittent startup failure seen on ConSLAM. They are the same event, at the same frame, every time. Section 07's experiment separates the two possible readings of that.
