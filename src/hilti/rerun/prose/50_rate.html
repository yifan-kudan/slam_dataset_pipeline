The estimate depended on how fast the bag was played

With clouds at 99.8 % and no gap over 0.10 s, site2_handheld_6 scored 11.1, 14.3 and 15.4 cm across three -r 0.3 runs and 54.9 and 55.7 cm across two -r 1.0 runs. Tight within a rate; four times apart between them. The earlier finding that "-r is not a knob" was about frame loss, which is writer-side and therefore genuinely rate-independent — it does not extend to the estimate.

Decomposed along FAST-LIO's own gravity state, against the surveyed vector between the two control points, the two rates disagree about different things on different sequences:

Which rig, not which sequence

Seven sequences were then run at both rates, chosen to span both rigs and to include three controls with enough surveyed points to be worth reading — 28, 21 and 15 control-point pairs, against the single pair on handheld_4. The pattern is not about which sequence is difficult. Every sequence that moved is on the 400 Hz handheld rig; the 200 Hz robot is identical to three significant figures.

That points at one line. FAST-LIO subscribes to /imu with a bare 10 — reliable KeepLast(10), which is 25 ms of buffer at 400 Hz — on the same single-threaded executor as a LiDAR callback that takes 4–8 ms per scan with spikes. When that queue overflows the reader loses IMU samples, and nothing anywhere logs it. Playing the bag at three-tenths speed does not make the node faster; it makes the arrival rate slower, which is why it looked like a rate effect. 

laserMapping.cpp -- a new parameter, default 10 = upstream
-   sub_imu_ = this->create_subscription<Imu>(imu_topic, 10, imu_cbk);
+   sub_imu_ = this->create_subscription<Imu>(
+       imu_topic, rclcpp::QoS(rclcpp::KeepLast(imu_queue)), imu_cbk);

At -r 1.0 with the deep queue, site1_handheld_5 returns 27.5 / 40.4 cm at 1.188 % relative — the -r 0.3 figures to four significant figures. So the two arms are not merely similar: with a queue deep enough not to overflow, real-time playback delivers the same input stream that slow playback did, and the estimate is the same estimate.

What the residual says

Two sequences do not close all the way — site3_handheld_2 lands at 5.2 cm against 3.8, and site2_handheld_4 at 59.3 against 22.6. The second is a one-pair score whose own scatter across runs of the same configuration spans 22.6 to 59.3 cm, so it cannot distinguish anything at that scale. The first is a real residual of about 1.4 cm on a 15-pair score, and the cloud subscription is the obvious remaining candidate: it is still upstream KeepLast(5), deliberately, because a reliable deep queue there cannot match a real LiDAR driver publishing best-effort. Not chased further — the whole dataset is now inside a centimetre of its slow-playback self, and the canonical run is at real time.
