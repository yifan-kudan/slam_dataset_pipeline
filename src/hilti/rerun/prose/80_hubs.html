08  one map per site

Coverage is a measurement, not the size of the map

Reusing one map across visits is the case that matters on a site: build once, localize on every later walk. Which of a site's sequences should supply that map is a question with an obvious wrong answer — the biggest map — and a measurable right one: the map that explains the most of the other sequences.

Those are not the same. Coverage here is the fraction of sequence B's cloud landing within 0.5 m of a point of sequence A's, after registering the two, and it is deliberately asymmetric: a small map inside a large one scores high in one direction and low in the other. Every ordered pair inside each site was registered — 20 for site1, 30 for site2, 12 for site3 — and the hub is the row with the highest mean.

Site 2 is the case that makes the point. Its hub is site2_robot_1, whose 1 m voxel count is fifth of the six — but it explains 0.70 and 0.69 of the two handheld clouds and 0.43–0.58 of the other robot runs, for a mean of 0.57. site2_handheld_6, second at 0.49, would also have been defensible. Ranking by map size instead would have picked site1_handheld_1 at site 1 (which the coverage measure also picks, at 0.47) but the wrong map at site 2.

Two other things the matrix says. The two site2 robot runs explain each other at 0.81 and 0.72 — far above anything else in the table — which is what two drives over the same ground should look like and a useful sanity check on the registration itself. And site1's numbers are the weakest of the three sites, 0.14 to 0.68, because its sequences overlap each other least: the walks share a building but not a route.

The prior, and how far to trust it

A hub map and another sequence's bag share no frame — each FAST-LIO map frame is its own rig's IMU pose at its own first scan — so every cross-sequence run needs initial_pose supplied. Levelling both clouds with the rotations align_map.py already measured collapses the search from six degrees of freedom to yaw plus translation, which is what makes this tractable: FFT phase correlation of wall-occupancy grids over a yaw sweep, a z-histogram correlation, then multi-scale trimmed point-to-plane ICP.

Overlap alone does not say whether the pose is right where the run starts, so two further checks do. The local check re-fits using only the points within 20 m of the sequence's own origin: if the global fit had been dragged by the far end of the site, this moves. Route coverage measures the same overlap in a 15 m ball around each of 200 poses along the sequence's own trajectory, so a map that runs out halfway shows up as a low minimum rather than a decent mean.

One registration the checks caught

site2_robot_2 into the robot hub has a perfectly ordinary overlap of 0.47 and a local check of 221 cm and 4.5° — an order of magnitude worse than any other pair. Its route coverage bottoms out at 0.15. The global fit is in the wrong basin near the start, and no overlap number would have told you: 0.47 sits mid-table. Nine of the twelve pairs land within 6 cm and 1°, which is what a usable prior looks like.
