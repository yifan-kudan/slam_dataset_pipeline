07  the covariance gate

Early on all eight, wrong on one of them

Two readings of section 06 are possible and they lead to opposite actions. Either the pose really is coming apart and the gate is doing its job, or the pose is fine and a mis-set threshold is throwing away most of eight bags. So: same map, same bag, same everything, max_pose_sigma_factor_increase 200 → 20000.

One row is a genuine false positive. site1_handheld_2 runs its full 166 s of 167 with no LOST at all, at a p95 of 19 cm and a worst frame of 67 cm — a completely healthy run that the gate had been cutting at 80 %. Its covariance factor peaks at 472, so a limit around 2.4× the current one would have kept it.

The other seven say the gate was early but not wrong. Given room, four of them diverge by tens to hundreds of metres — site2_handheld_4 reaches a p95 of 326 m — and three die on Motion predictor update failed, an uncaught non-convergence in the ESKF's iterated update rather than a diagnostic at all. site1_handheld_4 is worse with the looser gate than with the tight one, which is the clearest possible statement that removing the limit is not a fix.

Why the control points cannot arbitrate this

site1_handheld_1 scores 2.2 cm on its control points in the run whose trajectory ends up 514 m from FAST-LIO's. Its surveyed points all sit early in the bag, so the metric never sees the divergence. That is the whole reason for the dense comparison against the sequence's own odometry — 2 to 8 surveyed points cannot audit a 200 s trajectory, and a control-point score alone would have called this run excellent.

So the recommendation is a bounded one: max_pose_sigma_factor_increase of 200 is roughly 2.4× too tight for the handheld rig, and moving it to the 500–1000 band recovers a real run. Removing it is not an option, and the Motion predictor update failed path deserves a library ticket on its own — it kills runs that no threshold was consulted about.
