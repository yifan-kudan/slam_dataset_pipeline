04  odometry and maps

All fifteen on all of their data — and a second cause of loss

The shared-memory profile alone did not get there. On the first attempt the twelve handheld sequences came in at 99.6–99.9 % and all three robot sequences failed the gate: site2_robot_1 at 41.5 %, robot_3 at 71.2 %, robot_2 at 80.4 %. Two measurements say that was not the transport:

  • Per-frame LIO cost was 7.3 ms on the robot against 4.2 ms on the handheld rig — nowhere near the 100 ms budget, so it was not compute.
  • Binned over the run, robot_1 went 99.8 / 100 / 100 / 100 / 100 / 99.1 / 43.3 / 23.9 / 21.4 / 19.2 % by decile. Transport loss is flat in time. This ramps. And robot_2's log spans only 247 s of a 306 s bag — the run was truncated, not thinned.

publish_map() does two jobs. It appends the current scan to pcl_wait_pub, which is the cloud /map_save writes, and then it publishes the whole accumulated cloud on /Laser_map. So its cost grows with the square of the run length. site2_robot_1 accumulates 28.2 M points, so at map_period_ms: 370 its last messages are ~900 MB each, every 370 ms. That starves the node's own callback thread, the reader stops draining the shared-memory port, and the best-effort cloud writer drops — which is why the loss tracks the cloud rather than the clock. The handheld rig survived only because its clouds are sparser: 7.4 M points against 28.2 M, since blind is 1.0 m rather than the robot's 0.5 and the Bpearl is a short-range hemisphere whose near field is dense.

The saved cloud is unchanged — the accumulation is untouched — and nothing subscribes to /Laser_map in a headless batch run. With publish.map_publish_en: false in both Hilti configs and everything else identical, all fifteen re-ran at 99.6–100.0 %, robot_1 going from 41.5 % to 100.0 %.

A frame count is not a health check

site1_handheld_4 hit 99.8 % of its frames on the first attempt and still diverged outright — max |v| 273 m/s, a bounding box of 2463 × 1387 × 2457 m for a building 38 m across. With the publish disabled the same bag gives 2.05 m/s and 38 × 50 × 6 m. So the stall did not only drop clouds, it broke a run whose frame accounting was clean: the surviving frames arrive in bursts, and the estimate is not indifferent to that. Where the odometry stands

Eleven of the fifteen land between 1.7 and 16.2 cm of mean control-point error, and the well-sampled ones are in that group: site3_handheld_3 at 3.8 cm over 28 pairs, site2_robot_1 at 16.2 cm over 21, site3_handheld_2 at 5.2 cm over 15. That matters more than the headline figure, because a one-pair score is a single measurement — and this dataset has one-pair sequences whose scatter across runs of the same configuration is wider than the difference any of these fixes made.

Four do not. site1_handheld_5 at 27.5 cm and site2's three handheld walks at 59.3, 61.6 and 1295.3 cm are the sequences that cross building levels, and they fail the way this dataset has always failed: the vertical component is under-read by roughly a tenth per level, which survives every fix here. site2_handheld_5 is a different problem again — a ~37 m fold in the stairwell, which it has now produced at 50 %, 99.8 % and 99.8 % frames and at both playback rates alike (12.85, 12.89, 12.95 and 12.97 m mean pair error). Its map is built and reported anyway, because it turns the localizer section below into a test of what happens in a bad map — which is worth having.
